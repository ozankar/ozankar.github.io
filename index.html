GPU acceleration

Sparse Cholesky on GPUs

We offloaded dense operations within a right-looking sparse factorization. On the matrices we tested, factorization ran up to 4× faster than our CPU-only implementation.

Numerical algorithms

Pivoting for indefinite systems

I develop pivoting strategies for sparse symmetric matrices. A delayed pivot can change the factor’s structure mid-computation, affecting both numerical stability and memory use.

View the SIAM LA24 talk ↗

Distributed computing

Partitioning sparse tensors

I developed hypergraph models to distribute tensor operations across processors. The placement of nonzeros determines how evenly work is shared and how much data processors exchange.

Background

I’m Mustafa Ozan Karsavuran, a computer scientist based in the San Francisco Bay Area. I joined Berkeley Lab’s Applied Mathematics and Computational Research Division in 2022 as a postdoctoral scholar and became a Computer Systems Engineer 2 in 2025.

I earned my PhD in Computer Engineering at Bilkent University in 2020, working with Cevdet Aykanat on parallel sparse computations. Across this work, I use C, MPI, OpenMP, and dense linear algebra libraries to study where numerical software spends its time.
